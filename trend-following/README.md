# Time-series momentum across asset classes

Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen (2012) showed that an asset's own past twelve-month
return predicts its next month, in every major futures market, and that a
portfolio built on nothing but that sign does well exactly when equities do
badly. This is that strategy on free data: 15 markets in four asset classes,
2005 to August 2026, one signal, one sizing rule, one cost assumption.

- **Signal.** For each asset, the sign of its return from twelve months ago to
  one month ago (12-1). Long if positive, short if negative. No thresholds, no
  smoothing.
- **Sizing.** Each position is scaled to a 40% ex-ante annual volatility,
  using an exponentially weighted daily vol with a 60-day centre of mass, so a
  bond position is several times larger than a natural gas position.
- **Book.** Each of the four classes gets a quarter of the book, split equally
  among its live assets. Rebalanced at month end.
- **Execution.** A book set at the close of month t earns the return of month
  t+1. Costs are 5bps one-way on traded notional.

The short version: it works, it worked much better before 2010, it made money
in 2008 and 2022 while equities lost a third, and in 2020 it did nothing, which
is the interesting case.

## Data

| Class | Instruments | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Equities | ES=F, NQ=F, EFA, EEM | Yahoo continuous futures and ETFs |
| Bonds | ZN=F, ZB=F, TLT | Yahoo continuous futures and ETF |
| Commodities | CL=F, GC=F, HG=F, ZC=F, NG=F | Yahoo continuous futures |
| FX | 6E=F, 6J=F, 6B=F | Yahoo continuous futures |

All 15 series run from June 2003, giving a full 12-month warm-up before scoring
starts on 2005-01-31. `data.py` downloads once and caches to
`../source-material/trend-following/prices.csv`.

Yahoo's `=F` tickers are front-month splices with no roll adjustment: when the
front contract expires the series jumps to the next contract's price, and that
jump shows up as a return the strategy did not earn. Natural gas has 113 daily
moves over 10% in the sample and crude has 42, most of them roll gaps. The one
non-positive print in the sample is CL=F at -37.63 on 2020-04-20, which I treat
as missing (a percentage return off a negative price is meaningless; the next
day's -45% off the previous close is real for the expiring May contract, though
a real trend follower would have rolled out of it weeks earlier). Because of all
this the whole pipeline is rerun on an ETF-only universe with no splices, and
that is reported below as the cross-check.

## Design

| Window | Dates | Use |
|---|---|---|
| In-sample | 2005-01-31 to 2015-12-31 | Lookback comparison, all diagnostics |
| Final test | 2016-01 to 2026-08 | Reported once, untouched |

The headline specification (12-1 sign, 40% per-asset vol, class-balanced,
monthly, 5bps) was written down before any result was looked at. The lookback
comparison is a table, not a search: I report all five and did not pick from it.

**Look-ahead.** Every function in `trend.py` returns a value at month-end t
built only from prices up to t. The one-month lag lives in `backtest.run`, in a
single `shift(1)` on the weight panel, so it cannot be applied twice or missed.
`check.py` verifies this by mutation: it rewrites every price after a cut date
with random noise and asserts that every weight up to and including the cut is
byte-identical to the unmutated run, then asserts the weights after the cut did
change. A signal that peeks one month ahead, pushed through the same weighting
and execution code, scores Sharpe 13.7 on the synthetic panel where the real
pipeline scores 1.87.

**Formation and evaluation never overlap.** The 12-1 window ends one month
before the rebalance date and the return it is graded on starts the day after.

## Results

All numbers below are generated by `run.py`. Net is after 5bps one-way on
traded notional.

### Headline, full sample 2005-01 to 2026-08

| | Class-balanced (headline) | Equal-weight assets | 12-0, no skip |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual return, gross | +5.54% | +6.65% | +5.99% |
| Annual return, net | +4.87% | +6.04% | +5.34% |
| Annual vol | 16.65% | 16.04% | 17.01% |
| Sharpe, gross | 0.41 | 0.48 | 0.43 |
| Sharpe, net | 0.37 | 0.45 | 0.39 |
| Max drawdown, net | -33.40% | -27.82% | -34.37% |
| Drawdown trough | 2011-09 | 2012-10 | 2012-03 |
| Hit rate, monthly | 51.5% | 53.5% | 54.6% |
| Annual turnover | 12.8x | 11.5x | 12.5x |
| Avg gross leverage | 3.48x | 3.15x | 3.48x |

Against SPY over the same 260 months: beta +0.02, annualised alpha +5.88%.
SPY itself returned +10.90%/yr at Sharpe 0.78 with a -50.8% drawdown.

The equal-weight variant beats the headline because equities are the best
class and equal weighting gives them 4 of 15 slots instead of a quarter; I
keep the class-balanced version as the headline because that is what was
written down, and because "overweight the class that happened to trend best"
is not a rule I would trust forward.

| Period | Trend net | SPY | Bonds | Commodities | Equities | FX |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2008 full year | **+28.5%** | -36.8% | +9.7% | +0.2% | +6.8% | +10.0% |

| 2020 Feb to Mar (the crash) | +1.9% | -19.4% | +10.5% | +3.6% | -12.1% | +0.7% |
| 2020 Apr to Dec (the reversal) | -8.0% | +46.9% | -2.4% | -3.5% | +1.0% | -2.7% |
| 2020 full year | -2.3% | +18.3% | +13.7% | -0.7% | -12.5% | -1.1% |
| 2022 full year | **+30.5%** | -18.2% | +14.7% | +2.3% | -1.5% | +15.4% |

Class columns are gross contributions. Beta to SPY was -0.45 across 2008 and
-0.02 across 2020. In the 24 months where SPY fell more than 5%, the strategy
averaged +0.75% against SPY's -7.89%, positive in half of them.

2008 is the textbook case. Equities had been falling since late 2007, so the
book was short ES and EFA by June and short all four equity markets by the end
of August, three weeks before Lehman, and long Treasuries until the end of
November, when ZN and ZB flipped short for December (TLT stayed long).
Commodities added almost nothing (+0.2%) because the twelve-month window kept
the book long crude until the end of November, four months after the July
peak; copper flipped short at the end of September and that is roughly the
whole commodity contribution. 2022 is the same shape with the sign on bonds
flipped: short ZN, ZB and TLT from January into the fastest hiking cycle in
forty years, and short the euro, yen and sterling all year, which is long the
dollar.

The strategy was **long equities through the entire March crash**. The 12-1
signal at the end of February is the return from February 2019 to January 2020,
which was strongly positive, and the signal at the end of March (March 2019 to
February 2020) was still positive for ES and NQ. A twelve-month lookback cannot
see a five-week crash. The equity book lost 12.1% over February and March, and
the only reason the whole book was up 1.9% is that it was long bonds (+10.5%)
and, from the end of February, short crude and copper (+3.6%).

Then it flipped short ES and NQ at the end of April, one month after the
bottom, held that short through May while SPY rose 4.8%, and flipped back long
at the end of May. Vol scaling made the whipsaw smaller than it could have
been: ex-ante vol on ES roughly tripled in March, so gross leverage fell from
4.4x at the end of January to 3.8x at the end of February and 2.0x at the end
of March. But the shape is the failure mode of a slow signal in a fast
reversal, and it cost -8.0% from April to December while SPY made 46.9%.
Daily, the book bottomed on 2020-03-18 at -2.0% for the year and peaked on
2020-04-21 at +10.7%, so intra-month it looked better than the month-end
numbers; it then bled to -8.7% by 2020-11-10.

`reports/crisis_2020.png` draws this day by day with the month-end weights held
fixed inside each month, which is exactly what the monthly engine assumes.

### Decay

| Period | Sharpe gross | Sharpe net | Return net | Max dd | Hit rate | SPY Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2005-2009 | 0.65 | 0.61 | +8.3% | -20% | 47% | 0.11 |
| 2010-2014 | 0.28 | 0.25 | +2.8% | -25% | 57% | 1.17 |
| 2015-2019 | 0.30 | 0.26 | +3.1% | -27% | 47% | 0.98 |
| 2020-2026 | 0.43 | 0.39 | +5.2% | -30% | 55% | 0.94 |
| Post-2010 (all) | 0.35 | 0.31 | +3.9% | -30% | 53% | 1.00 |

Net Sharpe halved after 2009 and stayed there. The mean monthly net return was
+0.76% in 2005-2009 against +0.44% since, a difference of +0.32%/month with a
t-stat of 0.48, so the decay is what everyone has reported and also not
statistically distinguishable from noise on 260 months at 17% vol. The
2010-2012 stretch is three straight losing years (-6.7%, -10.3%, -7.2%), which
is where the maximum drawdown of -33.4% sits, and matches the industry-wide
"CTA winter" of the same years. 2022 pulled the recent period back up on its
own.

My reading is that the pre-2010 Sharpe of 0.6 was one long trend (the
2003-2008 commodity and equity run followed by its unwind) and the post-2010
Sharpe of 0.3 is what the strategy does in an ordinary decade. Neither window
is long enough to say which is the true number.

| Commodities | +2.20% | 0.42 |
| Bonds | +0.49% | 0.06 |

By asset, NQ, ES, yen and gold carry the book; the euro is the only material

### Costs

| One-way cost | Full Sharpe | In-sample | Test | Annual drag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0bps | 0.41 | 0.53 | 0.28 | 0.00% |
| 5bps (base) | 0.37 | 0.50 | 0.24 | 0.64% |
| 10bps | 0.33 | 0.46 | 0.20 | 1.28% |
| 20bps | 0.26 | 0.40 | 0.12 | 2.56% |
| 40bps | 0.10 | 0.26 | -0.05 | 5.11% |

Turnover is 12.8x of capital a year, which sounds high until you remember the
book runs at 3.5x gross, so it is about 3.7x of gross notional. Liquid futures
trade at 1-3bps, so 5bps is conservative for the futures and roughly right for
the ETFs. At 20bps the test-window Sharpe is 0.12 and at 40bps it is negative,
so this is a strategy that only exists in cheap markets.

The same pipeline on 14 ETFs (SPY, QQQ, EFA, EEM, IEF, TLT, USO, GLD, SLV, DBA,

| | Futures universe | ETF universe |
|---|---|---|
| Annual return, net | +4.87% | +8.24% |
| Sharpe, net | 0.37 | 0.52 |
| Max drawdown | -33.40% | -32.60% |
| 2008 | +28.5% | +24.3% |

| 2020 Apr-Dec | -8.0% | -6.4% |
| 2022 | +30.5% | +23.2% |

Monthly net returns correlate 0.88 between the two universes and every crisis
number has the same sign and similar size, so the roll splices are noise on top
of the result rather than the source of it. The ETF version is better, partly
because ETFs earn the risk-free rate and futures do not (a 3.6x-levered ETF book
is pocketing cash yield it would in reality have to fund), and partly because
several of its commodity and FX ETFs only list in 2006-2007 and so miss the
2005-2006 chop.

## How to run

```
../.venv/bin/python3 check.py
../.venv/bin/python3 run.py
```

`check.py` is offline and exits nonzero on any failure. `run.py` downloads once
to `../source-material/trend-following/prices.csv` and is offline after that.
No dependencies beyond the shared `requirements.txt`.

`data.get_panel(universe)` takes the `FUTURES` dict by default and `ETFS` for
the cross-check. Passing `"pit"` instead returns the point-in-time S&P 500
panel from `../pit-universe`, every name that was ever a member priced only on
its member days, all in one "equities" class. The default is unchanged, so
`run.py` reproduces the numbers above.

## Limitations

**The futures data is a splice.** Discussed above. Roll gaps are returns the
strategy did not earn (or lose), and the ETF cross-check is the mitigation, not
a fix. A real version needs back-adjusted continuous contracts.

**Futures and ETFs are mixed in one book.** Futures returns are excess returns;
ETF returns include the risk-free rate. At 3.5x gross leverage the ETF slots
(EFA, EEM, TLT here) earn cash yield they would have to borrow to fund. Over
2005-2026 that is on the order of 1-2%/yr of flattery on the three ETF slots,
less on the whole book. No financing cost is charged anywhere.

**Fifteen markets is a thin universe.** MOP use 58. With four equity, three
bond, five commodity and three FX contracts a single asset's bad year moves
the class number, and the class-balanced weighting puts a quarter of the book
on three currencies whose stand-alone Sharpe is 0.02.

**No survivorship problem in the usual sense, but a selection one.** These are
the fifteen most liquid contracts today, chosen in 2026. Nothing was delisted,
but I picked markets I already knew trend well (gold, Nasdaq, yen) and did not
pick ones I did not (softs, livestock, European rates). A universe chosen with
hindsight is a mild form of the same bias. For scale, the `pit-universe`
project measured what the same hindsight does on single stocks: an equal-weight
basket of today's S&P 500 members applied backwards beats the point-in-time
basket by +3.97%/yr in-sample and +4.23%/yr in the final test, a lower bound
because 257 of the 390 names ever removed from the index have no Yahoo prices.
Fifteen futures contracts that all still trade carry much less of that than a
stock list does, and I have not measured how much.
`data.get_panel(universe="pit")` loads that stock panel here for anyone who
wants to run the trend rule on it.

**Weights are fixed inside the month.** Positions are set at month end and held
flat; real positions drift with price and a real vol-scaled book would trade
intra-month as vol moves. In March 2020 that matters: the engine kept the 3.8x
book set at the end of February through the whole crash, where a daily-rebalanced book would have
cut it within days. The monthly number for March is therefore worse than a
daily implementation would show, and the April to December bleed is roughly
right.

**Costs are a flat rate.** No spread model, no impact, no roll cost, and one
number for a Treasury future and a natural gas future. The sensitivity table
shows the strategy dies somewhere between 20 and 40bps.

**The sample is one regime sequence.** 21 years containing one commodity
supercycle, one financial crisis, one decade of QE, one pandemic and one hiking
cycle. The decay finding in particular is 60 months against 200 and the
difference has a t-stat of 0.48.
